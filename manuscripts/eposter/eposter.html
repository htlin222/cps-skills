An Artificial Intelligence Clinico-Pathological Conference: Multi-Persona Bayesian Clinical Reasoning With Closed-Loop Self-Generated Bedside Safety Checks For Internal Medicine Training
Hsieh-Ting Lin, MD
Division of Hematology & Medical Oncology, Koo Foundation Sun Yat-Sen Cancer Center, Taipei, Taiwan

Closed-Loop Architecture

Background & Aim

Case-based teaching rarely makes explicit how much each finding should move a diagnosis. We built an AI clinico-pathological conference (CPC) that reproduces the NEJM Clinical Problem-Solving format while exposing every probability revision as an auditable likelihood ratio (LR) — and that audits itself: the errors it makes on one case become bedside safety checks constraining the next.

Methods

Knowledge base. 33 symptom-based chapters with LR values extracted from primary diagnostic-accuracy literature (McGee; Simel & Rennie).

Personas. Eight role-specific reasoners — attending, radiologist, pathologist, cardiologist, pulmonologist, infectious disease, neurologist and EBM specialist — activated by case-specific triggers, not run indiscriminately.

Inference. Sequential Bayesian updating on the odds scale (post-test odds = pre-test odds × LR; Fagan), retaining the full probability cascade for every competing hypothesis.

Self-audit. After the final diagnosis each phase is graded A–F against a checklist and every failure mode is rewritten as a reusable safety check; seven performance metrics are tracked across cases.

Key Result

Post-test probability can never exceed the probability mass assigned to the hypotheses actually on the list. Bayesian reasoning is bounded by the hypothesis space — so the teachable skill is not calibration, it is differential construction.

Conclusions

  1. An AI CPC makes diagnostic reasoning transparent and quantifiable, with an auditable LR cascade behind every probability it states.
  2. The retrospective-to-safety-check loop converts case failures into durable constraints, improving later performance without retraining.
  3. Trigger-based multi-persona rounds preserve subspecialty depth without indiscriminate breadth.
  4. Suitable for internal medicine training as a reasoning-visible adjunct to case conference — decision support, not decision replacement.
